We’ll read the receipt and fill in the details for you.

Upload your budget export (YNAB works — Date, Payee, Outflow, Memo). Reimbly turns each row into an expense and automatically attaches the matching receipt from your email. It flags duplicates and lets you review before anything is added.

What columns should the file have?

One row per expense, with a header row on top. Reimbly recognizes these columns (extra columns are ignored):

  • Date — best as YYYY-MM-DD (e.g. 2026-07-01)
  • Amount — the number only, in the original currency
  • Currency — USD, EUR, CZK, PLN, GBP, RON, HUF, BGN, RSD, UAH (defaults to USD)
  • Merchant — where it was spent
  • Description — what it was for
  • Account — the GL code (e.g. 8147000); leave blank to choose it here

Use “Copy instructions for Claude” to paste these rules into whatever puts your monthly file together.

Rules & reminders

Set the dollar amount below which a receipt isn’t required. Expenses at or under this go through without a receipt or a “no receipt” note; anything over still needs one. Foreign amounts are measured in US dollars.

Reimbursement deadline

How long staff have to submit an expense before it’s past the deadline. Reimbly gives a gentle reminder as each expense nears this — the first with 10 days to go, then closer as it counts down.

Accounts & access

The accounts people choose first on an expense (the ExpenseWire “Expense Type”). Add or rename them, retire the ones you no longer use, and set who may charge to each. An account with nobody assigned is open to everyone; once you add people, only they (and Finance) can use it.

People & access

Set everyone's role, upline (who approves them), which restricted accounts they can charge to, and which people share a household (pooled & reimbursed together, like a couple) — all from one spreadsheet. People are matched by email: existing ones are updated and new ones added (nobody is ever deleted).

What columns should the file have?
  • Name
  • Email — the key we match on (required)
  • Role — Staff, Approver, or Finance
  • Upline — the email of the person who approves them
  • Accounts — restricted GL codes they may use (e.g. 9100000, 9200000); leave blank for people who only use the normal open accounts
  • Household — a shared label for people whose expenses are pooled and reimbursed together, like a married couple; give both the same text, make it unique to that couple (e.g. Mel & Amy Ellenwood, not just a surname others share), and leave it blank for everyone else

Only list restricted general-fund accounts. Any account you grant to someone becomes visible to just those people; everything else stays open to all.

Email receipts

Optional. If you'd like, Reimbly can pull receipts straight from your email, so you don't have to snap or upload them. It's off unless you turn it on.

  • Reimbly never reads your inbox. A small helper runs inside your own Google account and only forwards emails that look like receipts or invoices.
  • Entirely your choice. Nothing happens until you switch it on, and you can switch it off — or remove the helper — anytime.
  • Just receipts. It looks for receipts and invoices, not your personal mail. Anything that isn't a receipt is skipped.

To set it up

  1. Turn on Email receipts in this menu.
  2. Add the one-time Gmail helper to your Google account (we'll send you the two-minute steps).
  3. New receipts show up in your receipt inbox on the Add-expense tab, ready to file into a report.

Not interested? Just leave it off — Reimbly won't touch your email at all.

Highlight receipts (date & total)

Re-reads each receipt image with the latest reader to mark where the date and total sit (so they’re highlighted when reviewing) and to fill in any receipt that’s missing a date. Dates you already set are left alone.

No receipt? Sign a declaration

Use this only when a receipt truly isn’t available (a market stall, a taxi, a tip). You’re certifying the expense is real — it then goes to your approver to sign off.
